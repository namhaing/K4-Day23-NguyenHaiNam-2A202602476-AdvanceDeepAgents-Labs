# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: A Survey

## TL;DR
- “Small” has no stable parameter cutoff: useful SLM definitions depend on capability and target hardware, while one 2025 survey scopes decoder-only models to 100M–5B parameters [1][2].
- Capability at low cost comes from balancing model size with data and training methods: Chinchilla’s 70B model outscored 280B Gopher by over 7 MMLU points at matched training compute, while DistilBERT retained 97% of BERT’s language-understanding performance at 40% fewer parameters [3][4].
- Inference bottlenecks are not only model weights: KV-cache management, sequence length, and serving schedules can constrain throughput; PagedAttention, cache compression, and self-speculative decoding target different parts of that system [5][6][7].
- Recent SLMs emphasize carefully composed synthetic and specialist data, but their reported capabilities do not yet constitute a standardized efficiency comparison: SmolLM2 uses 1.7B parameters and about 11T tokens, whereas Phi-4-Mini reports matching models twice its size on selected math and coding tasks [8][9].
- Evaluation needs quality and deployment cost together: local inference rankings vary by hardware and workload, and measured energy can differ substantially depending on whether CPU, DRAM, and disk are included [10][11].

## Background

Efficient inference and small language models are linked but distinct research problems. An SLM is not defined by one universally accepted parameter limit: surveyed definitions range from below 1B parameters to models as large as 10B, and a capability-relative definition ties “small” to the intended task and resource budget [2]. A complementary survey evaluates open-weight decoder-only transformers from 100M to 5B parameters, illustrating that the practical boundary depends on the comparison set and period [1]. The distinction matters because a model can be compact in parameter count yet still have high latency, memory use, or energy consumption under long contexts or concurrency.

Smaller models offer low latency and cost, customization, local data handling, and operation in resource-constrained settings, while larger models can impose cloud dependence and high inference costs [2]. DistilBERT used pretraining distillation losses combining language modeling, teacher distillation, and cosine distance; it was 40% smaller than BERT, retained 97% of language-understanding performance, and was 60% faster at inference [3]. Modern efficiency therefore concerns training, runtime memory, serving, and energy—not weights alone.

## Training and designing capable small models

Three levers recur: allocate more data to smaller models, transfer behavior from teachers, and compress representations. Chinchilla’s compute-optimal analysis covered over 400 runs (70M to >16B parameters; 5B–500B tokens) and concluded model size and training tokens should grow at approximately equal rates. At Gopher’s compute budget, a 70B model scored 67.5% on MMLU, over 7 points above 280B Gopher [4]. This supports reallocating training compute, not the claim that more data alone guarantees better inference.

Distillation transfers teacher outputs or internal distributions to a student. The SLM survey reports DistiLLM at roughly 1.5× vanilla-distillation training time, up to 2.3× faster than on-policy GKD, and with improved ROUGE-L on five instruction-following benchmarks [2]. A controlled synthetic study across four models from three families found reverse-KL on-policy distillation reliably transferred compositional skill to unseen reasoning structures but transferred minimal factual knowledge [12]. Teacher imitation therefore does not transfer every type of capability equally.

Quantization reduces the bits used for model state and, in some approaches, activations or the KV cache. LLM-QAT used 100,000 sampled examples on a single 8-GPU node and quantized LLaMA models at 7B, 13B, and 30B scales; in a 4-8-4 configuration it achieved 69.9 average zero-shot accuracy, 1.5 points below full precision [13]. The same report’s 8-8-4 result for 30B scored 69.7 versus 50.7 for SmoothQuant, highlighting the value of training-aware calibration under the tested setting [13]. These figures contrast with DistilBERT’s architectural reduction: distillation reduces the number of parameters, while low-bit quantization preserves a model’s broad architecture but reduces representation precision. They should not be treated as directly comparable scores because the model families and evaluations differ.

## Inference efficiency: weights, cache, decoding, and serving

The runtime bottleneck shifts with context length and workload. PagedAttention addresses dynamic KV-cache allocation: request caches grow and shrink, and fragmentation or duplicated state can waste memory and limit batch size. The method borrows virtual-memory-style paging for attention state and underpins vLLM [5]. KV-Compress instead removes under-utilized entries and applies variable compression rates per attention head, targeting the fact that cache memory grows with context length and limits concurrent long-context requests [7]. KVTuner takes a precision-allocation route, selecting sensitivity-aware, layer-wise mixed-precision quantization for the KV cache; its Hugging Face summary reports reduced latency and improved throughput without loss of effectiveness, but supplies no numerical benchmark values [14]. These approaches manipulate cache allocation, cache contents, and cache precision, respectively, and therefore address related but non-identical constraints.

A second class attempts to reduce serial decoding work. SWIFT adaptively skips intermediate layers in self-speculative decoding and is described as plug-and-play without an auxiliary model or extra training; the available summary gives no numeric speedup [6]. SpecExec instead describes massively parallel speculative decoding for consumer devices and RAM offloading, again without numeric throughput or quality results in the retrieved summary [15]. QuantSpec combines self-speculation with hierarchical 4-bit KV cache and weights for long-context edge inference, but the reported summary likewise lacks a quantified speed-quality tradeoff [16]. These reports suggest an appealing trend—integrating speculation with low-memory operation—but absent shared workloads and numbers, they cannot establish a ranking against one another.

System-level serving can dominate single-model kernel improvements. ScaleLLM’s summary reports a 4.3× speedup and 1.5× higher throughput than existing methods, while framing its contribution as end-to-end resource-frugal serving [17]. Those results should be read as framework-reported comparisons, not universal gains across hardware or traffic patterns. Bench360 demonstrates why operating conditions matter: its evaluation varies task, hardware, engine, quantization, and usage mode [10]. Efficient inference is therefore an interaction among model, cache policy, decoding algorithm, serving engine, hardware, and request distribution.

## Recent SLM design and reported capability

Recent model reports emphasize data curation and compute allocation alongside parameter count. SmolLM2 is a 1.7B-parameter model trained on approximately 11T tokens, with multi-stage mixtures of web text, mathematics, code, and instruction data; its report presents overtraining and data composition as central design choices [8]. Phi-4-Mini is 3.8B parameters and uses high-quality web and synthetic data, especially curated mathematics and coding examples; its report says it matches models twice its size on selected math and coding tasks [9]. These approaches share a data-centric rationale but differ in scale and evidence: SmolLM2 foregrounds a large token budget at 1.7B, while Phi-4-Mini makes explicit task-specific comparisons at 3.8B.

The same emphasis appears in larger “small” models and reasoning variants. Phi-4 is 14B parameters and strategically incorporates synthetic data throughout training; its technical report says it surpasses its GPT-4 teacher on STEM-focused question answering [18]. Phi-4-reasoning builds on Phi-4 with supervised fine-tuning on curated complex and diverse prompts and reasoning demonstrations generated using o3-mini; its “plus” variant adds a short outcome-based reinforcement-learning phase and is reported to perform better with longer reasoning chains [19]. This trades additional inference-time computation for reasoning performance, rather than assuming that every task should be served by the shortest possible decode.

At the other end, MobileLLM-Pro is a 1B model designed for on-device deployment with context windows up to 128,000 tokens and reports state-of-the-art results on 11 standard benchmarks, but the available abstract does not provide per-benchmark numbers [20]. The contrast with Phi-4’s 14B scale and Phi-4-Mini’s selected size-matched comparisons demonstrates why parameter count alone is an insufficient efficacy measure. These reports are promising evidence for data quality, specialization, and test-time compute, but direct comparisons of memory, latency, energy, and capability under common conditions remain sparse in the reviewed summaries.

## Evaluation: quality and deployment cost together

A useful evaluation suite must measure more than accuracy. HELM evaluates 30 language models across 42 scenarios and seven metric categories: accuracy, calibration, robustness, fairness, bias, toxicity, and efficiency; standardized evaluation increased core-scenario coverage from a prior average of 17.9% to 96.0% [21]. Yet its authors also identify coverage gaps, including question answering for neglected English dialects and trustworthiness measures [21]. For SLMs specifically, the 2025 survey collects 57 models and pairs capability tasks—commonsense reasoning, mathematics, in-context learning, and long-context retrieval—with on-device prefill/decode speed, memory, and energy measurements [1]. It uses a 50-token prompt and 50-token generation as its standard runtime test and takes measurements at 10-second intervals to mitigate thermal throttling [1].

Workload and measurement apparatus affect conclusions. Bench360 spans four tasks, three hardware platforms, and four inference engines, tracking latency, throughput, memory, energy, queueing, and task-specific quality [10]. Efficiency Pentathlon found CPU, DRAM, and disk accounted for over 60% of measured energy, cautioning against GPU-only reporting [11]. Benchmarks should disclose workload lengths, batch/concurrency, engine, hardware, quality, latency, memory, and energy boundary—not only tokens per second.

Evaluation cost can also be reduced without pretending that a small subset replaces full coverage. tinyBenchmarks reports that 100 curated examples can estimate MMLU’s roughly 14,000-example result with average error under 2% in its analysis; its reported reductions include 100 of 14K MMLU examples and 600 of 29K Open LLM Leaderboard examples [22]. The method can make iterative comparisons cheaper, but the cited error is an average estimation result, not a guarantee for every model or capability. Pairing economical screening with periodic full, deployment-relevant testing is a more defensible practice.

## Trends and open problems

Recent work emphasizes data-centric SLM design and integrated runtime efficiency: small-model reports foreground large token budgets and curated or synthetic data, while reasoning variants spend additional inference-time compute on harder tasks [8][9][19][20]. Cache paging, compression, mixed precision, speculative decoding, and end-to-end serving target different cost drivers [5][17][6][7][14][16]. Several Hugging Face-listed method summaries lack numerical speed or quality results, underscoring the need for comparable measurements [6][15][14][16].

Several open problems follow. First, there is no settled SLM size boundary, and a universal “best small model” ranking risks ignoring task and device constraints [1][2]. Second, comparisons need a shared accuracy–latency–memory–energy protocol: Bench360’s changing engine winners and the energy-boundary issue in Efficiency Pentathlon show how rankings can reverse with setup [10][11]. Third, evaluation has to reflect long contexts, concurrency, realistic prompts, and non-GPU power, rather than single-stream throughput alone [1][10][11]. Finally, distillation and synthetic-data recipes need more systematic assessment of what transfers—reasoning procedure, factual knowledge, or task-specific behavior—and at what added training and inference cost [2][9][12]. A credible efficiency claim should consequently state the quality retained, the exact workload and hardware, the system boundary, and the resource metric improved.
