# World Models: From Latent Simulation to Interactive, Embodied Predictors

## TL;DR

- “World model” names a family of systems, not one settled architecture: some predict action-conditioned latent states for control, while others predict abstract representations or generate future video, so claims need to specify the modeled state and tested use [1][2][3].
- Latent imagination has strong interaction-efficiency evidence: PlaNet matched a D4PG agent trained on 100,000 episodes after 1,000 episodes, while DreamerV3 reports one configuration across more than 150 tasks and eight domains [4][5].
- Generative approaches expand controllability and visual detail: Genie is an 11B-parameter model trained on unlabeled video, and DIAMOND reports 1.46 mean human-normalized score on Atari 100k [6][7].
- Evaluation is moving beyond image quality: WorldModelBench's correlation between rankings is 0.69 for frame-wise quality but 0.28 for physics adherence, while WorldArena finds perception scores correlate only 0.360 with action-planning performance [8][9].
- Long-horizon consistency, causal action understanding, physical calibration, and closed-loop utility remain distinct problems; benchmark results show strong performance on one dimension does not imply strength on the others [10][9][11].

## Background

A useful operational starting point is an internal model that represents latent aspects of an environment and supports prediction. In the classic “World Models” architecture, a visual encoder compresses observations, a recurrent memory predicts future latent codes, and a controller chooses actions; its predictive memory models uncertain futures with a mixture of Gaussians [1]. PlaNet made this idea explicitly useful for control: it learns a recurrent state-space model from images and performs online planning in latent space, avoiding the need to simulate every candidate directly in pixel space [4]. These definitions are related but not identical to predictive representation learning. I-JEPA predicts representations of masked image regions rather than reconstructing their pixels, while the 2025 operational-definition paper distinguishes emergent representations of latent world state from explicit action-effect models [12][3].

The concept matters because a learned predictor can, in principle, let agents test candidate actions without paying for every real interaction. LeCun’s proposed architecture assigns the model prediction of plausible futures and missing state information, while a separate cost module evaluates those futures and an actor proposes actions [2]. This separation is important: predictive accuracy, visual plausibility, and decision usefulness are different properties. The survey therefore groups approaches by what they model and how they use it, then compares them against benchmarks that increasingly test those properties separately.

## Latent dynamics and imagination for control

Latent model-based reinforcement learning compresses observations into a manageable state, predicts its evolution under actions, and uses simulated trajectories to improve decisions. Dreamer’s defining move is to learn behavior through latent imagination from images; its paper summary emphasizes data efficiency, computation time, and final performance compared with prior approaches [13]. PlaNet offers evidence for the interaction-efficiency argument: it reports using substantially fewer episodes than strong model-free algorithms while reaching comparable or sometimes higher performance [4]. Its results support the value of latent planning in the tested control setting, not a universal guarantee that model-based methods require fewer interactions.

DreamerV3 extends this family across a much wider task range. It uses a learned environment model, critic, and actor to imagine future scenarios and reports a single fixed configuration on more than 150 tasks in eight domains; it also reports collecting diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula [5]. Its evaluated model sizes ranged from 12M to 400M parameters, with larger models associated with higher task performance and lower interaction requirements [5]. Compared with PlaNet’s six-task efficiency evidence, DreamerV3 emphasizes breadth and robustness across domains. Both make action-conditioned latent prediction instrumental to a controller, unlike a purely visual generator. But their success depends on learned dynamics remaining reliable over imagined rollouts: PlaNet itself identifies compounding multi-step errors, missing multiple futures, and overconfident predictions outside training distributions as problems for planning [4].

## Generative video and controllable environments

A second family learns to generate future observations, often using video and action-like controls. GAIA-1 frames driving as unsupervised sequence modeling conditioned on video, text, and actions [14]. Genie instead learns latent actions from unlabeled Internet video: its 11B-parameter system combines spatiotemporal tokenization, autoregressive dynamics, and a latent-action model, enabling controllable environments from unlabelled videos [6]. The technical report also describes a parameter-scaling study that preceded the final 11B model [15]. That is a different route to action conditioning from GAIA-1: learned latent actions avoid requiring ground-truth action labels in the source video, whereas GAIA-1's inputs explicitly include actions [14][6][15].

Pixel-level fidelity can also be an intentional design choice. DIAMOND uses diffusion as an Atari world model and argues that compressed discrete latent sequences can discard visually important details; on Atari 100k, covering 26 games and 100,000 environment actions per game, it reports a 1.46 mean human-normalized score and 0.64 interquartile mean, outperforming human players on 11 games [7]. By contrast, Dreamer-family approaches prioritize compact latent rollouts for policy learning, rather than high-fidelity pixel simulation [5][13]. Genie 2 extends prompt-conditioned generation to playable 3D environments: its announcement reports consistency for up to one minute, with most examples lasting 10–20 seconds, while explicitly noting room to improve consistency and agent/environment generation [16]. These systems demonstrate increasingly controllable generation, but generation alone does not establish causal understanding or usefulness for closed-loop control.

## Embodied and long-horizon world models

Recent embodied work couples video prediction with physical or action representations. RoboScape jointly learns RGB video generation and physics-related representations using temporal depth prediction and keypoint dynamics; it trains on 50,000 videos covering 147 tasks and 72 skills, preprocessed into about 6.5 million training clips [17]. Its model-size comparison—34M, 131M, and 544M parameters—reports significant improvement across all six evaluation metrics as capacity increases; the paper also reports that generated data improves Diffusion Policy and pi0 [17]. LongScape addresses a different bottleneck: it combines diffusion within chunks with autoregressive generation between chunks, and reports visual coherence over 15 rollouts plus average improvements of 8.6% on LIBERO and 5.8% on AGIBOT-World over its strongest comparison baseline [18]. RoboScape emphasizes physics-related supervision and downstream policy data; LongScape emphasizes chunking and context-aware experts for temporal continuity [17][18].

Genie Envisioner broadens this integrated direction with a video diffusion model, an action decoder, and an action-conditioned neural simulator; its text reports over one million manipulation episodes and 54-step torque trajectories generated within 200 ms on a commodity GPU [19]. It reports adaptation to Dual Franka using 250 teleoperated episodes and a unified model trained on 200 demonstrations outperforming π0 and GO-1 on three of four RoboTwin tasks [19]. An emerging multi-agent direction targets synchronized first-person streams with fine-grained interaction, rather than coarse locomotion, camera control, or discrete commands [20]. Collectively these reports shift the target from plausible video toward action-conditioned prediction and embodiment transfer. However, separate success metrics and task setups make direct ranking across systems inappropriate; the core question is whether generated futures support reliable policies in unseen tasks and embodiments [17][19].

## Evaluation: realism is not utility

Benchmarks increasingly separate perceptual fidelity, physics, planning, interaction, and downstream utility. WorldModelBench evaluates video models not only for visual quality but also for adherence to physical laws, using human labels to assess whether generations violate physical principles [8]. This is a more targeted question than whether a clip looks convincing, and motivates evaluating physical consistency separately from generic video quality [8]. WorldScore evaluates 20 models on 3,000 examples with ten metrics; it finds video models weaker than 3D/4D approaches for camera controllability and long-sequence generation, with the best reported video camera-control score at 40.22 [21]. Together, these results caution against treating general video quality as physical correctness or controllability [8][21].

Causal planning tests reveal another gap. WorldPrediction asks models to infer an action connecting initial and final states or to order actions into a plan, using counterfactual distractors and action equivalents. Its strongest reported results were 57.0% for the world-modeling task and 38.1% for procedural planning, while human participants solved both perfectly; video-diffusion baselines reached 30.1% and 26.1% on the respective reported comparisons [10]. WorldArena shifts from perception to functional utility, testing world models on robot-manipulation scenarios and assessing perception alongside functional tests such as data synthesis, policy evaluation, and action planning [9]. It reports EWMScore in relation to human judgments, while its separate downstream tests underline that perceptual assessment and functional utility are different evaluation targets [9]. Thus improved human-rated perceptual quality is useful but is not a substitute for measuring policy or planning outcomes [9].

Interactive testing is also exposing temporal degradation. WBench contains 289 test cases and 1,058 interaction turns across five dimensions; across 20 models, navigation is weakly correlated with video quality (r = −0.12), consistency (r = −0.05), and physical compliance (r = −0.15) [11]. Its analysis reports a 33-point navigation-accuracy drop between turn one and turn four or later, with compounding pose errors, while event editing and subject action scores fall 13 and 9 points [11]. WorldBench separately finds that plausible-looking trajectories can still miss physical parameters such as gravitational acceleration (9.8 m/s²), and that models show high variance across repeated gravity estimates and struggle more with viscosity than friction [22]. These evaluations show why a single score cannot stand in for a world model's complete capability profile [22][11].

## Trends and open problems

Over the recent period represented in these sources, the field has moved from compact latent simulators for RL toward large video-trained generators, followed by efforts to attach explicit action, geometry, physics, and policy-evaluation components [5][6][15][17][19]. Scale is promising but not a solution by itself: Genie reports a parameter-scaling study, and RoboScape reports metric improvements from 34M to 544M, yet evaluations still find failures in physical calibration, long-horizon planning, or downstream utility [15][17][10][9][22]. The newest evaluation evidence calls for capability-specific assessment: WorldModelBench, WorldPrediction, WorldArena, and WBench measure distinct aspects, and their reported correlations show these aspects need not move together [8][10][9][11].

Three open problems follow from these results. First, uncertainty and compounding errors must be controlled across long rollouts; PlaNet identifies both, while WBench observes navigation accuracy deteriorating over successive turns [4][11]. Second, models need stronger causal and physical generalization: WorldPrediction remains far below human performance on procedural planning, and WorldBench finds plausible motion without reliable physical-parameter estimates [10][22]. Third, evaluation must connect generated content to closed-loop outcomes across tasks, embodiments, and interaction horizons; WorldArena's weaker relationship between perceptual score and planning performance and Genie 2's stated consistency limitations make that gap explicit [16][9]. A credible world model should therefore be judged not only by what it renders, but by what it predicts under interventions and what an agent can reliably do with those predictions [2][9][11].
